3 · The brief for whoever draws the cat

  1. Species: cheetah, not tiger. Cheetahs are built long and low — the silhouette twists into a letterform. Tiger stripes fight the linework at icon size; cheetah spots reduce to dots that survive.
  2. Pose: stalking crawl, three-quarter view coming toward the viewer. Shoulder blades up, head below the shoulder line, one forepaw extended. Eye contact with the viewer — that's the "cool girl" part.
  3. Line: single-weight ink outline, 2.5px at 200px tall, round caps and joins. No shading, no gradient, no fill texture. Spots as solid ink blobs, six to nine of them, not a pattern.
  4. The twist: the tail is one continuous stroke that leaves the hindquarters, crosses behind the body, and becomes the right diagonal of the Y. It must read as tail at the start and as letter at the end — the crossover point is the whole trick.
  5. The S: the tail tip curls over the top-left of the S and terminates in a filled dot (the dot from the current app icon). One tail, two letters, one gesture.
  6. Sizes it must survive: 1024px (App Store), 180px (home screen), 60px (settings row), 24px (favicon). At 24px only the Y and the dot survive — that's why direction D exists.
  7. Deliverables: SVG, single layer, outlined strokes, transparent background. Plus a solid one-colour version for embroidery and stamps.
  8. Do not: add a face expression beyond eyes and a mouth line, add a collar, add motion lines, or put the cat inside a circle badge — it should break the frame, not sit in one.
