The plate order · for the insulin
Same food. Different order.
Your insulin is still high. The cheapest lever you have doesn't change what's on the plate, only the order. Vegetables and protein first. Starch last. Ten minutes between them if the meal allows; if not, just make the rice the last thing your fork touches.

In 16 adults with type 2 diabetes eating an identical meal three ways, carbohydrate-last produced a 53% lower glucose rise and about 25% less insulin than carbohydrate-first (Shukla, BMJ Open Diab Res Care 2017).

Two honest caveats. When the same group tested it free-living for 16 weeks, HbA1c didn't move. And part of how it works is by slowing stomach emptying — which is exactly what tirzepatide already does, so you may be getting less of this than someone not on the medication.

Do it anyway, for a different reason: eating the protein first is how you actually finish it on a day your appetite is gone. Protein you finish is muscle you keep.
The ten-minute walk
01.

After dinner, every night

In 41 adults with type 2 diabetes, ten minutes of walking after each meal lowered post-meal glucose about 12% versus the same total walking done once a day — and about 22% after the evening meal, the carbohydrate-heaviest one (Reynolds, Diabetologia 2016). The overall figure only just cleared significance; the dinner result is the solid one. Which is convenient, because dinner is the easy one to build a habit around.

02.

Two minutes beats zero

Short light walking breaks through a sitting day lower both glucose and insulin, and beat standing on both (Buffey, Sports Medicine 2022). The trials cluster around 2 minutes every 20, or 5 every 30. Quality was rated fair-to-good and the authors flagged possible publication bias — so treat the direction as solid and the exact number as soft.

03.

The desk version

Three minutes of half-squats, calf raises and glute squeezes every half hour matched walking on every glucose and insulin measure, and beat it on triglycerides (Dempsey, Diabetes Care 2016). For meeting days when a walk isn't happening.

Iron · pair it or lose it
Why pairing beats portions
Iron comes in two forms that behave completely differently. The haem iron in red meat absorbs at roughly 15–35% and barely cares what else is on the plate. The non-haem iron in spinach and beans absorbs at roughly 2–20%, and that range is decided almost entirely by what you eat and drink alongside it.

Which means red meat is doing most of the real work, and the plant sources need help to count for anything.

01.

Vitamin C with every plant-iron meal

About 50 mg alongside it — roughly half a red bell pepper, which already lives in your rotation. Ascorbic acid pulls non-haem absorption up sharply and helps override the blockers in the same meal. A squeeze of lemon is not enough.

02.

Coffee and tea an hour away

Polyphenols are the strongest blocker on the list — single-meal studies put black tea's inhibition of non-haem iron in the 79–94% range and coffee in a similar band. Your breakfast is now the iron breakfast, so coffee moves to after it. The 2 PM cutoff still stands.

03.

Milk shakes at a different sitting

Calcium and iron compete in the same meal. Honest version: this shows up clearly in single-meal studies but has been hard to demonstrate as an effect on iron status over months. Don't reorganise your life around it — just don't drink the shake alongside the steak.

04.

Meat lifts the plants beside it

Animal muscle improves absorption of the non-haem iron sitting next to it. Beef with spinach beats spinach alone by more than the arithmetic suggests. Build iron meals as meat-plus-greens, not either one solo.

05.

About spinach, honestly

Spinach's reputation outruns its performance — absorption has been measured as low as about 2%. Keep it for the folate, the volume, and because it makes a plate look finished. Don't count on it to move your labs.

Labs · what to ask for
Ferritin needs company
Ferritin rises with inflammation all on its own. In a controlled infection study it nearly doubled in three days and returned to baseline by day seven — in people whose actual iron stores could not have changed. So as your inflammation settles, a ferritin number can move without your iron moving.

Your doctor picked the better marker. Transferrin saturation is iron actually in transit, and under inflammation it falls at the same time ferritin rises — which is what makes the pair diagnostic.

Ask for ferritin, transferrin saturation and CRP drawn together, and compare them against your pre-treatment values. One more thing worth knowing before a "normal" result reassures anyone: the standard cutoff is under review, and by newer physiologically-derived thresholds roughly a third of women test deficient where the old threshold says a fifth do.
!

Iron pills are not yours to start

Supplemental iron without confirmed deficiency can do real harm — your body has no active way to get rid of an excess. Your labs, your doctor, her call. This page is about food.

?

One question to bring

Are you on a reflux medication — omeprazole, pantoprazole, esomeprazole, or an over-the-counter version? They're commonly added alongside GLP-1s, and they're an established cause of poor iron absorption. That may matter more to your iron than the tirzepatide does.

Why the cards say what they say
Reference · iron
Getting the iron in is not the problem. Absorbing it is.
the pairing rules, and how much to trust them
Beef, eggs, spinach, beans and chickpeas were all on the list your doctor gave you — but only the beef carries haem iron, the form your gut takes without argument. Everything else is non-haem, and how much of it you actually absorb swings by a factor of several depending on what is on the plate beside it. That swing is what the iron rules on each card are managing.
The four rules, and the number behind each
  • ~50 mg vitamin C in every iron meal. Across 299 subjects, absorption rose in direct proportion to ascorbic acid; 50 mg per main meal was the stated point of practical optimum. Half a bell pepper, one orange, or 80 g of broccoli each clear it. Hallberg, Brune & Rossander 1986, Hum Nutr Appl Nutr 40:97
  • A little meat or fish lifts the plants. Against an equal amount of egg protein, beef muscle raised non-haem absorption 180% and chicken 100%. This is why 2 oz of beef sits in a chickpea breakfast. Hurrell, Reddy, Juillerat & Cook 2006, J Nutr 136:2808
  • Coffee and tea after, never with — and before is free. Coffee cut absorption 39% and tea 64% from the same meal. The timing finding is the useful one: coffee an hour before the meal did nothing at all, coffee an hour after inhibited as much as drinking it alongside. Morck, Lynch & Cook 1983, AJCN 37:416 · Hallberg & Rossander 1982
  • Phytate is the reason beans underdeliver. 25 mg of phytate-phosphorus cut absorption 64%; 250 mg cut it 82%. Rinsing tinned pulses, and soaking dried ones, both help — and vitamin C directly counteracts the inhibition rather than just adding to it. Hallberg, Brune & Rossander 1989, AJCN 49:140
Two things you have probably read that are wrong
  • Oxalate is not what makes spinach a poor iron source. It was tested head-to-head: 16 women, spinach (1.27 g oxalic acid) against kale (0.01 g), then kale with oxalate added to match. Absorption 10.7% vs 11.5%, p=0.86 — no effect. Spinach is still a mediocre source, but because of its calcium and polyphenols, not its oxalate. Bonsmann et al. 2008, Eur J Clin Nutr 62:336
  • The "you only absorb 2% of spinach iron" figure has no traceable source. It is repeated everywhere and attributed to nothing. It is not printed on any card here.
How much to trust all of this. Every dramatic number above comes from a single labelled test meal in a fasting subject. When the same questions are asked over a whole diet, the effects shrink — one study spanning 51 to 247 mg of vitamin C a day found no significant difference in absorption at all (Cook & Reddy 2001), and the 2010 reference-values review calls these effects "modest in varied, multimeal studies" (Hurrell & Egli). The rules are directionally right and cost nothing to follow. They are not a 3× multiplier on your ferritin. The lifting, the red meat and the actual iron dose are doing the heavy work; the pairings are trimming the losses.
Reference · insulin
Insulin is the open problem. Here is what actually moves it.
ranked by how good the evidence is, not by how good it sounds
Inflammation is improving and the elimination work is doing its job. Insulin resistance is the thing still standing. These cards are built around the interventions with the best evidence in PCOS specifically — which turns out not to be the ones the internet talks about most.
Strong · meta-analysed, hard endpoints, replicated
  • Beans, lentils and chickpeas as the carbohydrate. A cup of legumes a day for three months lowered HbA1c by 0.5% against a high-wheat-fibre control in 121 people, a between-group difference of 0.2% (p<0.001). Backed by a 41-trial meta-analysis. This is the best-evidenced single change on the whole list — and it is the same change that raises your iron. Jenkins 2012, Arch Intern Med 172:1653 · Sievenpiper 2009, Diabetologia 52:1479
  • Viscous soluble fibre, around 13 g a day. Across 28 comparisons in 1,394 people: HbA1c −0.58%, HOMA-IR −1.89, moderate certainty. That HbA1c effect is roughly half a metformin dose-step, from food. Jovanovski 2019, Diabetes Care 42:755
  • Low glycaemic index carbohydrate, in PCOS specifically. Ten RCTs, 403 women: HOMA-IR −0.78, total testosterone −0.21 nmol/L, waist −2.81 cm. And the 12-month RCT behind it found menstrual cyclicity improved in 95% of the low-GI group versus 63% of controls. This is why quinoa replaced white rice and why zucchini ribbons replaced pasta. Kazemi 2021, Adv Nutr 12:161 · Marsh 2010, AJCN 92:83
  • Fewer, larger meals — your three-meals-no-snacking pattern is the right one. Two large meals beat six small ones at identical calories over 12 weeks: more weight lost, more liver fat lost, and greater improvement in insulin sensitivity (p=0.01). Kahleova 2014, Diabetologia 57:1552
  • Saturated fat is the thing to avoid; monounsaturated fat is the replacement, not a treatment. Over three months at matched calories, a saturated-fat diet impaired insulin sensitivity by 10% while a monounsaturated one held it flat. Read that carefully — olive oil and avocado did not improve anything, they avoided the damage. The benefit also disappeared entirely once total fat went above 37% of energy, which is why these cards are not drowning in oil. Vessby 2001, KANWU, Diabetologia 44:312
Directionally sound but thinner than it sounds
  • Eating order — vegetables and protein first, carbohydrate last. The acute effect is big and repeatedly replicated: glucose area-under-curve 38–73% lower, insulin AUC significantly lower, and it works in prediabetes as well as diabetes. But the total number of people across all those trials is under a hundred, none of them had PCOS, and the one meta-analysis of longer trials found no HbA1c benefit (−0.21%, confidence interval crossing zero, low certainty). It costs nothing, it survives a 16-week trial at 94% adherence, and it makes you eat the vegetables first — which may be most of the point. Treat it as a free habit, not a treatment. Shukla 2015 & 2017 · Okami 2022, BMJ Open Diab Res Care 10:e002534
  • Front-loading calories into breakfast. The single most striking PCOS result found: 60 women, same 1,800 calories, moved from dinner to breakfast for 90 days — insulin AUC fell 54%, free testosterone fell 50%, SHBG doubled, ovulation increased. One trial, never replicated at that size, and done in lean PCOS. Big enough to be worth trying, thin enough that it should not be treated as settled. Jakubowicz 2013, Clin Sci 125:423
  • Omega-3. Three separate meta-analyses in PCOS put the HOMA-IR effect at −0.80, at −0.57, and at nothing at all. The anti-inflammatory and triglyceride effects are far more consistent than the insulin ones. Oily fish twice a week is still right — just not for the insulin.
On the 120 g protein target. Your doctor set it, and these cards deliver it — three meals of 40–48 g. Worth knowing what sits behind the number: PCOS trials that tested high protein used about 30% of energy, which lands near 120 g at your intake, and they disagree with each other about whether it improves HOMA-IR at all. What does hold up is body composition — a high-protein arm lost 1.5 kg of fat-free mass against 4.4 kg on standard protein. And the intervention with the strongest evidence for protecting muscle on tirzepatide is not protein at all, it is resistance training: lean mass was 17.5% of total weight lost with lifting versus 25.4% without. THE GOVERNING PRINCIPLES

Ten rules. Read once a month.

Programs change. The principles don't. When you're tempted to skip, to add, to chase a new method — come here first.

The Ten
01.

Protein protects what the cut threatens

On GLP-1s, your body will lose weight either way. Protein + lifting decide whether you lose fat or muscle. 120–130g daily, every day, no exceptions.

02.

Show up five times — even when small

The med makes you tired some days. A 30-minute version of the workout that gets done is worth more than the perfect one you skip. Log it either way.

03.

Progressive overload, always

Each week, add weight, reps, or quality on the priority lifts. The Log tab tells you what last week looked like. Beat it by something.

04.

Hips, core, balance every week

Wednesday is the dedicated day. But Bulgarian splits, dead bugs, Pallof presses, and single-leg work weave through every other day too. The midsection follows.

05.

Hydration is non-negotiable

Tirzepatide suppresses thirst. Drink 2.5L water minimum. Add electrolytes on lift days. Headaches and fatigue are usually dehydration, not effort.

06.

Fresh food, not stored

Histamine builds in storage. Cook portion-size. Eat within 24h if you must save. Freeze immediately for longer.

07.

Sleep first, supplements last

7+ hours minimum. Caffeine cutoff 2 PM — yes, even on stimulant days. Creatine 5g daily is the only supplement worth the line item.

08.

The scale lies weekly, tells truth monthly

Weigh daily, look at the 7-day average. Sodium, period, glycogen, water — they all add 1–4 lb noise. The 4-week trend is the real signal.

09.

Photos beat the scale

Photos every 4 weeks, same morning, same light, same suit. Waist-tape too. These tell the truth when the scale doesn't.

10.

Consistency outscores perfection

Four good workouts a week for 50 weeks beats five perfect workouts for 12. 80% adherence beats 100% for two weeks then quitting.
